When talking about net neutrality, I think it is important to distinguish facts from myths. In this seminar, we are discussing net neutrality as a principle that promotes fair and equal access to online content, regardless of users' locations or political views. More specifically, net neutrality requires governments to enforce regulations on Internet Service Providers (ISPs) within their respective countries to ensure that there is no unfair discrimination or prioritization of internet traffic for individual companies, whether they pay for it or not.

First, many consumer protection groups and, quite surprisingly, many large companies also support net neutrality. For example, in January 2024, Netflix publicly supported restoring net neutrality regulations in the United States. The company submitted comments to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), arguing that internet service providers could unfairly slow down competing streaming platforms to promote their own services. Netflix argued that net neutrality helps ensure streaming platforms compete fairly without ISPs favouring their own content. Despite being a major company that could potentially afford prioritized internet traffic, Netflix's decision to support net neutrality in 2024 provides us with an important insight: even large companies can benefit from a fair internet connection for all. Furthermore, net neutrality allows smaller businesses and startups to compete without having to pay additional fees for faster internet delivery.

Conversely, ISPs could be among the biggest losers if strict net neutrality regulations were fully enforced, since these rules aim to restrict providers' power to prioritize bandwidth for individual services or companies willing to pay for preferential treatment. However, ISPs argue that such regulations could limit their revenue opportunities and discourage investment in network infrastructure.

If net neutrality protections are removed, many argue that individual users can seek out other ISPs to receive the best quality and access to the internet. However, in an internet without net neutrality protections, this poses a major risk of ISPs adopting similar pricing and prioritization practices, potentially leaving consumers with few affordable alternatives. This is especially concerning in rural areas where there may be only one or two available internet providers.

Personally, we believe net neutrality affects everyone, as the internet has become an important resource for people to seek out information, enjoy entertainment, and, most importantly, work. Gone are the days when most workplaces had no internet requirements. Net neutrality has an important role in preventing ISPs or large organizations from slowing down or outright blocking internet connections for financial gain. That is why I think net neutrality is important for the sake of everyone's right to fair internet access.

The abolition of net neutrality, in the form we discussed above, could have serious consequences on a societal scale. We could argue that without net neutrality, money would have greater influence over internet accessibility, while individual rights could be overlooked. That is why we think net neutrality is NOT a form of censorship but quite the opposite. Nevertheless, net neutrality only addresses certain forms of censorship or discrimination by internet providers, not censorship imposed by governmental bodies. I strongly believe that protections against government censorship and excessive surveillance should also be considered in the broader goal of achieving a truly free and open internet.
